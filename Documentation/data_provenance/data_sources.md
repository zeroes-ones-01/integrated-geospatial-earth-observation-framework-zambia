# Data Sources, Provenance and Methodological Notes

**Project:** An Integrated Geospatial Database and Earth Observation Framework for Assessing Agricultural Productivity, Climate Vulnerability and Agricultural Greenhouse-Gas Emissions in Zambia

**Study period:** 1991–2020

**Documentation status:** Source-to-cleaned-data comparisons have been completed for the selected FAOSTAT emissions observations and the CRU climate series. Further source metadata and processing details remain to be documented.

## 1. Overview

The current national statistical database contains agricultural, climate and selected greenhouse-gas emissions observations for Zambia covering 1991–2020.

The identified underlying data sources are:

1. FAOSTAT, for agricultural statistics and selected greenhouse-gas emissions indicators.
2. Climatic Research Unit (CRU) TS v4.10, as the underlying climate dataset identified by the downloaded climate workbook.

The climate workbook was downloaded through the World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal (CCKP). The CCKP is therefore acknowledged as the confirmed distribution platform, while CRU is identified as the underlying climate data provider. These roles are distinct and should not be conflated.

The present document distinguishes verified source-to-file correspondence from metadata and processing details that have not yet been independently established.

## 2. FAOSTAT

### 2.1 Agricultural statistics

The project contains agricultural records for 11 crops, covering production, harvested area and yield from 1991 to 2020.

Original FAOSTAT downloads located during source verification include:

- `FAOSTAT_data_en_7-23-2026 (1).csv`
- `FAOSTAT_data_en_7-16-2026.csv`

These files contain crop and livestock product observations and fertiliser-by-nutrient observations, respectively.

The project also contains cleaned agricultural datasets for production, harvested area, yield, crops and fertiliser nutrients. Source attribution should be maintained at the individual indicator and field level, including the original indicator, unit, geographical coverage and extraction period.

The source-to-cleaned-data and database integrity work should be interpreted in conjunction with the project's data-integrity documentation. The presence of cleaned records alone does not replace the need to preserve the original downloads and their metadata.

### 2.2 Selected greenhouse-gas emissions

The original emissions source file is:

`FAOSTAT_data_en_7-16-2026 (1).csv`

The source records were filtered using the following criteria:

- Area: Zambia
- Element code: 723113
- Element: Emissions (CO2eq) (AR5)
- Years: 1991–2020

The extraction produced 120 records, comprising four categories with 30 annual observations each:

- Crop Residues
- Rice Cultivation
- Burning - Crop residues
- Synthetic Fertilizers

All extracted records identify `FAO TIER 1` as the source, use `kt` as the unit, and carry the flag description `Estimated value`.

A record-by-record comparison against `data/processed/emissions/co2_equivalent.csv`, matching Item and Year, found:

| Verification measure | Result |
|---|---:|
| Original source records | 120 |
| Cleaned records matched | 120 |
| Mismatched or missing records | 0 |
| Source values preserved | Yes |
| Source units preserved | Yes |

The cleaned dataset therefore reproduces the selected FAOSTAT observations exactly.

### 2.3 Interpretation of emissions values

The selected FAOSTAT element is already expressed in carbon-dioxide equivalents under the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report (AR5) global-warming-potential framework.

Consequently, the project imports and analyses the published CO2-equivalent values. It does not apply methane or nitrous-oxide global-warming-potential factors again to these observations. Applying a second conversion would incorrectly transform values that have already been expressed in CO2-equivalent terms.

The source classification and estimation status must be retained in interpretation and reporting. These observations are FAO TIER 1 estimates, rather than measurements independently generated by this project.

The four selected categories must not be interpreted as a complete inventory of Zambia's agricultural greenhouse-gas emissions. Conclusions drawn from them must be restricted to the selected indicators and their documented coverage.

The database stores the emissions unit as `kt`. In this project, this represents kilotonnes of CO2-equivalent, consistent with the selected FAOSTAT element.

## 3. Climate data: CRU TS v4.10 and the CCKP download

### 3.1 Underlying dataset and distribution platform

The climate workbook was downloaded through the World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal (CCKP).

The source file is:

`cru-x0.5_timeseries_tas,pr_timeseries_annual_1901-2025_mean_historical_cru_ts4.10_mean.xlsx`

Its filename identifies the underlying climate product as CRU TS v4.10 and indicates a 0.5-degree climate-data product.

The workbook contains two worksheets:

- `pr` — precipitation
- `tas` — temperature

Each worksheet contains one Zambia record, identified by the code `ZMB` and the name `Zambia`, with annual columns covering 1901–2025.

Attribution must distinguish the CCKP as the confirmed distribution platform from the Climatic Research Unit, University of East Anglia, as the underlying climate dataset provider.

### 3.2 Distinguishing CRU-TS and CRU-CY

CRU-TS and CRU-CY are related but distinct products:

- **CRU-TS v4.10** is a gridded time-series climate dataset covering global land areas at 0.5-degree resolution for 1901–2025.
- **CRU-CY v4.10** is a separate country-averaged time-series product derived from CRU-TS.

The workbook used in this project contains one national Zambia record in each of its `pr` and `tas` worksheets. It therefore provides a national-level annual series for the study period, rather than provincial observations or individual grid-cell records.

The workbook should not be labelled CRU-CY unless its product metadata or download record confirms that it is the CRU-CY product. Its filename and worksheet structure alone do not establish that identity.

The distinction matters because the underlying CRU-TS product is gridded, whereas the workbook supplied to this project contains a single national record. The workbook should therefore be described according to the data actually analysed, not as though the project directly analysed the underlying grid cells.

### 3.3 Source-to-cleaned-data verification

The workbook was compared with:

- `data/processed/climate/Rainfall.csv`
- `data/processed/climate/Temperature.csv`

All years from 1991 to 2020 were checked individually.

| Variable | Worksheet | Matched observations | Mismatches |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Precipitation | pr | 30/30 | 0 |
| Temperature | tas | 30/30 | 0 |
| **Total** | | **60/60** | **0** |

The comparison used a numerical tolerance of less than 0.000001. All 60 annual values matched the cleaned datasets within that tolerance.

This establishes numerical correspondence between the downloaded workbook and the cleaned CSV files. It does not independently validate the original climate observations, reconstruct the national aggregation algorithm or establish the complete processing history of the workbook.

### 3.4 Units, temporal interpretation and spatial limitations

The workbook identifies the variables as precipitation (`pr`) and temperature (`tas`). The expected interpretation is annual precipitation in millimetres and annual mean temperature in degrees Celsius. These units should be confirmed against the version-specific download metadata before being treated as definitively verified attributes of this particular workbook.

The CCKP's currently published observed-climate metadata describes CRU-TS v4.08 and documents a workflow involving gridded climate data, spatial aggregation to administrative and basin units, and the preparation of annual climate summaries. It describes annual precipitation as accumulated precipitation and annual temperature as an average.

This metadata provides useful context for understanding the portal's documented approach. However, because the published metadata identifies v4.08 rather than the v4.10 version used in this project, it does not independently establish the precise processing workflow applied to the project's workbook. The exact aggregation procedure, boundary version, area-weighting method and version-specific processing steps remain to be confirmed.

The workbook contains a single national Zambia series rather than provincial observations or a collection of spatial grid cells. It can support descriptive national-level historical climate analysis, but it cannot independently support provincial or district-level climate comparisons or spatial mapping.

In particular, national annual values cannot resolve:

- Within-country differences in rainfall and temperature.
- Seasonal rainfall distribution and growing-season conditions.
- Short-duration rainfall extremes and dry spells.
- Local drought exposure or temperature extremes.
- Spatial differences in agricultural climate vulnerability.

These limitations must be addressed in the later spatial and Earth Observation phase through appropriately documented datasets with suitable spatial and temporal resolution.

## 4. World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal

The World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal was the platform used to obtain the climate workbook.

The project attributes the underlying climate dataset to CRU TS v4.10 and acknowledges the CCKP as the distribution platform. The CCKP should not be presented as the original producer of the CRU observations.

The portal's published observed-climate metadata is useful for understanding its general climate-data processing approach. However, its currently published metadata identifies CRU-TS v4.08. It must therefore be cited with that version qualification and should not be used as conclusive evidence of the exact processing applied to the project's v4.10 workbook.

No specific World Bank indicator series has yet been demonstrated to contribute records to the current database. Any additional World Bank data attribution must be supported by the relevant indicator identifier, source metadata and corresponding database records.

## 5. Data provenance, reproducibility and outstanding verification

The original FAOSTAT downloads and CRU workbook were located in the external downloads directory. The repository's `data/raw/` directory was empty during the source inspection. The original FAOSTAT downloads and CRU workbook were maintained in the external downloads directory rather than within the repository.

The source-to-cleaned-data comparisons establish numerical correspondence for the selected emissions and climate records. However, full repository-level reproducibility requires the original source files, source metadata, extraction criteria and processing records to be preserved in an appropriately documented location.

The following details remain to be documented or verified:

1. Original download URLs and access dates for each source file.
2. The exact CCKP product page and version-specific metadata associated with the downloaded climate workbook.
3. Authoritative variable-unit metadata and calculation definitions for the `pr` and `tas` series.
4. The national aggregation method used to produce the Zambia climate series.
5. Exact source indicator identifiers for any additional data attributed to the World Bank.
6. Applicable source licensing and redistribution conditions.
7. The relationship between each original source file, its cleaned counterpart and the corresponding database table.

Original source files should be preserved unchanged and distinguished from cleaned and transformed datasets. Any future cleaning, transformation or aggregation should be documented through reproducible procedures, with sufficient information to trace analytical records back to their source.

## 6. Scope and interpretation

The current database supports national-level analysis of selected agricultural production, harvested area, yield, climate and greenhouse-gas emissions indicators for 1991–2020.

The four emissions categories represent selected FAOSTAT indicators rather than a comprehensive agricultural greenhouse-gas inventory. Their estimation status and Tier 1 source classification must remain explicit in subsequent analysis.

The climate observations are national annual values. They do not resolve within-country climate variability, seasonal rainfall distribution, drought events or local temperature extremes.

Trend estimates and correlations derived from these data are descriptive and should not be interpreted as proof of causal relationships. The temporal coverage and aggregation of the available observations also constrain the strength and scope of statistical inference.

The planned spatial database and Earth Observation components are separate stages of the project. Their data sources, spatial resolution, temporal coverage, processing methods and validation procedures will be documented when those stages are undertaken. No spatial or Earth Observation results are claimed in this source documentation.
