# Problem Statement

## 1. Background

Agriculture is central to Zambia's food security, rural livelihoods and economic development. Agricultural production is nevertheless influenced by interacting environmental and socio-economic conditions, including rainfall variability, temperature conditions, land availability, soil characteristics and agricultural input use. At the same time, agricultural activities contribute to greenhouse-gas emissions through processes such as crop-residue burning, rice cultivation and the application of synthetic fertilisers.

Understanding these relationships requires reliable integration of agricultural, climatic and emissions data. Geospatial information and Earth observation offer additional opportunities to examine the spatial distribution of agricultural production and environmental conditions. However, these different forms of information are often maintained in separate datasets and analytical environments, making integrated assessment difficult.

## 2. Problem Definition

The principal problem addressed by this project is the limited integration of agricultural productivity statistics, climate information and agricultural greenhouse-gas emissions within a coherent, reproducible analytical framework for Zambia.

National agricultural and environmental datasets can support the examination of long-term patterns, but their effective use depends on consistent data structures, documented provenance, appropriate units, validation procedures and transparent analytical methods. Without these foundations, comparisons across datasets and the interpretation of observed relationships may be unreliable or difficult to reproduce.

A further limitation concerns geographical resolution. National annual statistics describe broad temporal patterns but cannot adequately represent differences between provinces, districts, farming systems or individual agricultural landscapes. National averages may also conceal seasonal variability, extreme events and localised environmental pressures. Consequently, national statistical analysis alone cannot establish the spatial distribution of agricultural climate vulnerability.

For this project, climate vulnerability is understood as a context-dependent condition shaped by climate exposure, the sensitivity of agricultural systems and their capacity to adapt. These dimensions should not be treated as interchangeable. The current national database provides selected information relevant to agricultural productivity and climatic conditions, but it does not yet contain a sufficiently developed set of indicators to measure adaptive capacity. Accordingly, the current work does not constitute a comprehensive assessment of agricultural climate vulnerability.

## 3. Research Gap and Rationale

There is a need for an integrated framework that brings together agricultural productivity indicators, climate records and selected agricultural greenhouse-gas emissions while establishing a sound foundation for subsequent geospatial and Earth observation analysis.

This project addresses that need through the development of a structured geospatial database and an associated analytical framework. Its initial implementation establishes a national statistical baseline for 1991–2020, supported by data harmonisation, relational database design, validation and exploratory analysis.

The planned geospatial and Earth observation component will extend this foundation by investigating spatial patterns and environmental conditions using appropriately selected spatial datasets and satellite-derived information. This extension is necessary because the national statistical analysis cannot, by itself, resolve local or subnational patterns of exposure and agricultural vulnerability.

## 4. Research Aim

To develop an integrated geospatial database and Earth observation framework for assessing agricultural productivity, climate vulnerability and agricultural greenhouse-gas emissions in Zambia.

## 5. Research Objectives

The project has the following objectives:

1. To identify, acquire and document relevant agricultural, climatic, emissions and geographical datasets for Zambia.
2. To harmonise and integrate selected national agricultural, climate and emissions statistics for the period 1991–2020.
3. To design and implement a relational database that supports structured storage, data integrity, traceability and reproducible analysis.
4. To examine temporal patterns in agricultural production, harvested area, crop yields, climate variables and selected agricultural greenhouse-gas emissions.
5. To investigate descriptive relationships between agricultural productivity and climatic conditions while recognising the limitations of observational national-level data.
6. To establish a framework for integrating geospatial datasets and Earth observation products to support subsequent subnational environmental assessment.
7. To identify data limitations, methodological uncertainties and opportunities for improving the spatial and analytical resolution of the framework.

## 6. Scope and Boundaries

The current statistical database covers Zambia for the period 1991–2020. Its implemented components include selected crop production, harvested-area and yield indicators, national annual precipitation and temperature series, fertiliser nutrient statistics, selected agricultural emissions indicators and provincial reference information.

The emissions component represents selected categories available from the source data. It must not be interpreted as a complete national greenhouse-gas inventory.

The current climate series are national annual observations or summaries. They do not provide sufficient information to establish provincial or local climate exposure, the distribution of rainfall within the growing season, dry-spell duration, drought intensity or crop-relevant heat extremes. Assessing these conditions requires temporally and spatially appropriate climate information, interpreted in relation to relevant agricultural calendars and crop-development stages.

The project is being developed in stages. The current implementation establishes the database, documents data provenance and examines national statistical patterns. Spatial analysis, vegetation monitoring, drought and heat mapping, and the integration of Earth observation products are planned extensions. These activities must be supported by appropriate spatial data, documented processing procedures and independent validation before spatial findings are reported.

The project is observational and analytical. Correlations and temporal associations are not, by themselves, evidence of causation. Conclusions must therefore remain proportionate to the temporal coverage, geographical resolution, completeness and quality of the available data.
